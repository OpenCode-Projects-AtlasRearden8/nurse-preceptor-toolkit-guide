# Documenting Precepting Hours

**Education only — this guide is never medical advice.** This chapter covers habits and hygiene for keeping your own record of precepting hours, skills observed, and milestones — for your memory, your orientee's confidence, and the educators who depend on both. It is not clinical guidance and never overrides your facility's documentation policies. **Where your facility's official form and this guide disagree, the facility's form wins, every time, without exception.**

Nobody loves this part. The furthest thing from anyone's Friday is a log. And it costs one honest hour a season, repays you when an auditor, an educator, or a manager says "show me" — and repays everyone the moment you can answer "how far along are they, actually?" without hand-waving.

## Why the log carries weight (yours, theirs, everyone's)

- **For you.** Feedback [chapter 3](03-feedback-delivered-kindly.html) says behavior-specific; a log that is a week stale can only be vibe-specific. Hours and skills-written-down are the difference between *"he's coming along"* and *"he closed the routing loop independently on Tuesday, called the charge nurse for the fork cleanly Wednesday."*
- **For them.** An orientee in month three who has lost faith in their own progress needs receipts more than encouragement. Your log becomes their proof: *"here is what you could not do in week one, and here is Tuesday."*
- **For the educators.** Unit educators juggling multiple preceptees can only work from what reaches them. Clean weekly summaries make their job possible, make your orientee's reality real, and make you the preceptor nobody has to chase.
- **For the employer's form-of-record.** Nothing here **replaces** anything. It feeds it, prepares it, and saves your wrists from retyping.

## The three-tier system

One tier per cadence, one worksheet per tier that matters. The system is "one row, occasionally" — not a diary.

| Tier | When | What goes in | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 — hours | Every shift, 60 seconds | Date, shift, hours precepted, independence level (watch / side-by-side / checked / independent) for the day overall | [Precept hours log](../worksheets/precept-hours-log.html) |
| Tier 2 — skills & wins | 2–3× per week, 3 minutes | Any specific skill observed, any moment that went well, any moment you stepped in (see [chapter 2](02-teaching-without-rescuing.html)), words verbatim when verbatim matters | Same worksheet, lower section |
| Tier 3 — the weekly summary | Once per week, 10 minutes | Hours total, rungs moved, barriers, next week's focus, anything an educator should see | [Weekly teaching plan](../worksheets/weekly-teaching-plan.html) plus a copy for the educator, if your unit uses one |

Total honest time: about an hour a week for the first month, about 30 minutes a week once tiers establish themselves. Everything here is *yours*: general worksheets written for any unit become, once filled, personal work products.

## Independence levels — the vocabulary that makes logs useful

Borrow the four words from [chapter 2](02-teaching-without-rescuing.html)'s ladder and use them *consistently*:

- **Watched** — you demonstrated or performed the task, narrating; the orientee remained hands-off (rung 1–2).
- **Side-by-side** — both of you engaged; you did half or less, checking live (rung 2–3).
- **Checked** — orientee performed the whole task; you observed or verified at fixed checkpoints (rung 3–4).
- **Independent** — orientee completed the task with no real-time involvement from you; you learned about it in debrief (rung 4–5).

A week that reads *watched→watched→checked→checked→checked* tells an educator more than a paragraph. A "verified/independent" milestone is a sentence any auditor can read without an interpreter.

## The weekly summary, in 10 lines or fewer

Ten minutes on the last shift of the week, formatted for pasting into whatever your facility's actual process uses:

```
Week [N] of [unit orientation length]. Orientee initials only: [__]
Precepted hours this week: [__]   Cumulative: [__]
Rungs moved: [task A: checked → independent] [task B: side-by-side → checked]
Wins worth telling the orientee: 1) __ 2) __
Barriers (no names of staff/patients): __
Next week's focus: __
Educator needs to know: __
```

Send it (per your unit's actual process) and put a copy in your [hours log](../worksheets/precept-hours-log.html). Two minutes, and it ages into primary-source material for the eventual [readiness summary](09-handoff-readiness-summary.html) — week eight's document starts here, which is the quiet trick of this whole chapter.

## What belongs where (privacy fence)

- **Worksheets (yours, paper or personal files):** behavior descriptions, verbatim words, hours, rungs. Fine.
- **Facility systems (theirs):** whatever your facility's policy says goes there. Follow it; when unsure, ask the educator which of your observations they want and where.
- **Nowhere, ever:** trainee names on shared paper beyond your own copy's necessity, patient identifiers anywhere on personal paper, "funny story" content within earshot of any colleague, photographs of anything, and — say it with feeling — **anything patient-identifying in any AI tool ever** (see [chapter 5](05-ai-for-lesson-plans.html)).

The precept-hours-log worksheet is designed with this fence pre-installed: an initials-only header, no name fields, and a banner reminding you the sheet is *your* memory aid, not a chart.

## When hours and reality disagree

Sometimes the required hours for an orientation milestone arrive (say, 160 scheduled) while the demonstrated independence hasn't. Handle it as information, not verdict:

1. **Say it plainly first, to your orientee** — [chapter 1](01-setting-a-new-grad-at-ease.html)'s honesty rules outrank schedule-embarrassment ESPECIALLY here: "the hours are checking off on paper and the independence isn't; we're going to use the remaining time differently, together."
2. **Escalate via the right channel, on time** — your unit educator, using words from your log (hours-vs-milestones, no adjectives).
3. **Resist fixing it with overtime rescue** — the [difficult week trap](08-surviving-the-difficult-week.html) adds hours without adding readiness, and quietly fails everyone in the third act.
4. **Update the readiness summary honestly** — the [chapter 9](09-handoff-readiness-summary.html) form has both fields by design: hours *logged* and readiness *demonstrated*, because they are two different facts and everyone downstream needs both stated.

## The one-line summary

One row per shift, one sentence per win/step-in, one ten-line summary per week — a log honest enough to be useful to every audience downstream (you, them, educator, employer) while never claiming to replace any of them.
