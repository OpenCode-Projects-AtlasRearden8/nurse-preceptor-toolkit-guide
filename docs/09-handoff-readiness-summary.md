# Handing Off With a Readiness Summary

**Education only — this guide is never medical advice.** This chapter covers the end-of-precepting season: the final weeks' shape, the one-page readiness summary, and the farewell that closes it. It teaches documentation and communication habits only; formal competency validation, scheduling, and release-to-independent-practice decisions belong to your facility's designated process and owners, and nothing here overrides any of it.

The last week mirrors the first: on day one you gave them the map and the safety net. In the last week you take the net down, hand them the map they have been quietly drawing all along, and say something true about what they built. Finished well, the last week is the best-teaching week of the whole rotation.

## The final week's shape

| Day | Theme | Plan |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Full autonomy, live | They carry the assignment at rung 5 ([chapter 2](02-teaching-without-rescuing.html)); you are a colleague who happens to check in at lunch |
| Tue | The quiet audit | You observe one full shift quietly; note everything for the summary — wins AND the honest edges |
| Wed | Their teach-back day | They teach you their best two skills, and they run one [dry-run scenario](06-dry-run-scenarios.html) *for you* as the practice caller |
| Thu | The readiness conversation | You two read the summary draft together, their margins first (below) |
| Fri | The clean handoff and the true farewell | Summary signed via whatever your facility's process is; the human goodbye |

## The readiness summary: one page, two honest halves

Your facility will have its official competency paperwork — none of it is yours to validate, and it stays theirs. The summary you write and share is the *coaching* document that lives beside it: one page, addressed to the orientee and readable by the educator downstream without translation. Build it all season with tiered notes from [chapter 7](07-documenting-precepting-hours.html); the blank form is [readiness-summary.md](../worksheets/readiness-summary.html) in the worksheets.

**Half one — demonstrated independence.** Facts with dates, from the log:

- Skills or tasks this orientee carried at rung 4–5 with dates ("full run organization, independently, since week six").
- The autonomy rung they reached per task family by the final week.
- Escalation behavior observed — the single most downstream-readable line in the document: *"when unsure, asked early and clearly, times observed: [n]."*

**Half two — continuation coaching.** The true edges, behavior-first ([chapter 3](03-feedback-delivered-kindly.html)'s bar/gap/attempt shape, one line each):

- Two behaviors still on watch, phrased as the next bar ("end-of-shift handoffs at the independence rung was the shortest muscle; continue stating the ask first").
- One habit worth protecting, named so it survives the first solo-assignment slide.
- One stretch for their next ninety days, chosen *with* them, small and concrete.

Then the two fields that must be separate because they say different things: **hours logged** and **readiness demonstrated** ([chapter 7](07-documenting-precepting-hours.html)'s honest disagreement section). Both facts, both stated, no adjectives gluing them.

## The Thursday conversation — their margins first

Hand the draft over and let them read in silence, then:

1. **"What did I get wrong or undersell?"** Their self-knowledge beats your co-observation of eight weeks; where they are harder on themselves than you were, argue *their* corner with the receipts.
2. **"Which line would you want quoted on a bad day in month three?"** That line — theirs, not yours — becomes the farewell line, and it's the one they'll remember.
3. **"What should the educator hear from you directly in your own words?"** Handing off the narration is the final act of the whole thing: the summary is your record of their becoming, but they are the ones who own it now.

## The farewell, done honestly

Three parts, five minutes, and none of it needs to be moving (the moving takes care of itself):

1. **What you observed, one true specific** — the thing you will tell the educator about them (already in your log, which is the point of having kept one).
2. **What you want from them** — not gratitude: *"Tell me in six months how month three went. Genuinely: I'm asking."* A standing offer beats a framed certificate; one checkout conversation at ninety days is worth more than any speech.
3. **The door line** — "My number's where you have it [per your facility's policy on contact]; bad day, big win, or dumb question: all three are open invitations." (Or the local equivalent if policy says preceptor contact ends — then the door line is a name: *"Your [educator's role] knows exactly where you are and wants the same updates."* Either version is a real door; both count.)

Then — this is the part everyone gets wrong — **let them carry the last word.** The orientation was theirs; its closing sentence should be too. Whatever they say last, receive it without editing and let them walk off the shift as the author, which by the last Friday they fully are.

## Handing the educator a good handoff

One paragraph, two minutes, from your real material ([chapter 7](07-documenting-precepting-hours.html) weekly summaries, assembled):

> "Here's [initials]'s eight weeks in one view: independent at [task family A/B] since week [n], autonomy rung summary attached, one watch-behavior named with the coaching plan tested, one protected habit, hours-vs-readiness both stated. Ask me anything."

That paragraph, for a real educator, is worth ten of the best-oriented meetings: the orientee who arrives *documented* is the orientee who starts their first solo week on catches, not guesses — and both the orientee and the educator will remember that it came from you.

## The one-line summary

The last week takes the net down and ends with everyone still standing: a one-page summary in two honest halves, a Thursday conversation they narrate, a Friday with their words last — a clean handoff that makes the next preceptor's-or-educator's job easier and the orientee's first solo week safer.
