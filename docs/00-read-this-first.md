# Read This First

**Education only — this guide is never medical advice.** This manual teaches coaching habits, conversation scripts, planning worksheets, and documentation ideas for nurses who are asked to precept a new grad. It does not teach clinical care, does not evaluate anyone's competence, and never overrides your employer's policies, your state's nurse practice act, your unit's educator, or your clinical team. If a situation touches patient safety, your facility's protocols and escalation chain come before anything written here.

## Who this is for

- Any nurse who said (or reluctantly typed) "fine, I'll precept" without a manual in hand.
- Experienced nurses who are excellent clinicians and are discovering that excellent clinicians are not automatically excellent teachers.
- Preceptors near the end of a long orientation who need their next two weeks to feel plan-able instead of panic-able.

It was written in plain English, step by step, the way a friend would explain it — never condescending, no jargon without a translation (the glossary is [chapter 10](10-glossary.html)).

## The one-paragraph philosophy

Precepting is not doing the shift with a shadow. It is transferring a job from someone who can do it to someone who almost can, on purpose, in the smallest safe steps, with the truth told kindly at every step. You are the bridge, not the railing and not the roof: you hold weight for a while, and you are designed to be outlived.

## How to use it

1. Skim the whole thing once before your orientee's day one, so nothing catches you flat-footed.
2. Fill the worksheets in week one (start with [preceptor-setup](../worksheets/preceptor-setup.html)), then maintain a [weekly teaching plan](../worksheets/weekly-teaching-plan.html) — fifteen minutes of planning buys back hours of derailed shift.
3. When AI comes up at work (it will), read [chapter 5](05-ai-for-lesson-plans.html) before you paste anything anywhere.
4. Reread [chapter 8](08-surviving-the-difficult-week.html) whenever your weather turns. Rough weeks are a named phase with a known shape, not a verdict on your teaching.

## Ground rules this toolkit assumes

- **Nothing here validates or *fails* anyone.** Formal competency sign-offs belong to your facility's process and its designated owners.
- **No patient specifics, ever, on paper or in an AI chat.** Personal paper and personal AI tools get no names, dates, room numbers, or details that could identify a person.
- **AI output is a first draft you owe a fact-check.** Chapters 5 and 6 are built around verification, not vibes.
- **The paper trail follows policy.** The [hours log](../worksheets/precept-hours-log.html) and [readiness summary](../worksheets/readiness-summary.html) carry information; your facility's official forms remain the record of record.

## The one-line summary

Nobody precepts perfectly on day one — including you. Your job is not to make a perfect nurse in perfect order. It is to hand over a demanding job in safe increments, say true things kindly, and build someone who no longer needs you, which is the whole point and also the thank-you.
