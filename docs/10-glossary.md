# Glossary: The Words Recruitment Forgot to Explain

**Education only — this guide is never medical advice.** Definitions here are plain-English paraphrases of the *precepting* vocabulary you'll hear this season — coaching-culture words, not clinical ones, and not technical or legal definitions. They are never a substitute for your facility's policy definitions, your state's nurse practice act, or your educator's program materials.

You learned forty acronyms to survive your own first year; nobody handed you the teaching vocabulary that arrives with a preceptee. This is the translation layer.

## Role and program words

| Term | Plain meaning | Practical note |
|---|---|---|
| Preceptor | The experienced nurse formally paired with a learner at the bedside | Your role per this handbook; scope is set by your facility's preceptor program |
| Preceptee / orientee | The nurse you're paired with — usually a new grad, sometimes an experienced transfer | Two very different starting points; ask which and adjust |
| Orientation (unit) | The structured period during which a new hire learns the unit, supervised | Length and shape are your facility's; you coach inside it |
| Unit educator / CDU / NE | The nurse whose *whole job* is education and competency process | Your best ally; loop them in early ([chapter 8](08-surviving-the-difficult-week.html)) |
| Charge nurse | The shift's traffic controller | Your first escalation step for most day-to-day questions |
| Manager / director | Operates the unit, owns staffing and process outcomes | Conversations there are structural, not coaching |
| Precepting hours | Time officially logged as orientation supervision | Your facility's form is the record ([chapter 7](07-documenting-precepting-hours.html)) |
| Competency validation | The facility's formal process of confirming skills | Not yours to perform; yours to *support* with observations |

## Coaching-method words

| Term | Plain meaning | Practical note |
|---|---|---|
| Graduated autonomy | Handing over responsibility in planned, increasing steps | The autonomy ladder: [chapter 2](02-teaching-without-rescuing.html) |
| Think-aloud | Speaking your reasoning while doing a task, on purpose | Rung 1's engine; every demonstration should carry it |
| Formative feedback | Coaching feedback meant to develop, not grade | Chapters [3](03-feedback-delivered-kindly.html) and [7](07-documenting-precepting-hours.html) live here |
| Summative evaluation | The formal end-of-period judgment | Not this handbook's; your facility's process owns it |
| Role-play / dry-run scenario | A fictional, clearly-labeled practice exercise | Chapters [4](04-growing-critical-thinking.html) and [6](06-dry-run-scenarios.html) |
| Teach-back | The learner explains or demonstrates back in their own words | The cheapest verification you own |
| Debrief | The structured few minutes after an attempt: what happened, why, next | Four-question format in chapter 6 |
| Question ladder | Asking recall-to-reasoning questions in a planned climb | The six rungs: chapter 4 |
| Scaffolding | Temporary support that intentionally comes down | The whole philosophy of this book in one word |
| Socratic questioning | Questions that lead the learner to their own reasoning | Question rungs 3–6, asked kindly |

## Document words

| Term | Plain meaning | Practical note |
|---|---|---|
| Timesheet / preceptor log | Whatever your facility uses to record precepted hours | [Chapter 7](07-documenting-precepting-hours.html)'s tiers feed it, never replace it |
| Readiness summary | A one-page coaching summary of demonstrated independence + continuation coaching | Your worksheet, beside the official paperwork: [chapter 9](09-handoff-readiness-summary.html) |
| Handoff / report | Transferring a patient's care between nurses | SBAR is the standard structure; teach it early |
| Milestone | A named checkpoint (often tied to weeks or hours) | Hours and readiness can disagree — say both honestly (chapter 7) |
| Escalation | Taking a concern to the next level of support | "I'm uncomfortable" is a complete sentence (their first-year guide taught them this too) |

## Words of caution, translated

| You'll hear | It usually means | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| "They're just not ready" | Someone needs dates and behaviors, not moods | Bring the log: hours, rungs, examples ([chapter 7](07-documenting-precepting-hours.html)) |
| "Sink or swim" | A staffing-era myth, not a teaching method | Swim lanes are built, not thrown; chapters 1–2 are the build |
| "They were never cut out for it" | A shortcut through a complicated conversation | Mismatch gets handled with educators and evidence, chapter 8 |
| "We always did it this way" | A local habit worth checking against policy | Verify before you teach it ([chapter 5](05-ai-for-lesson-plans.html)'s rule number two) |
| "Just document it" | Someone wants the trail without the care behind it | Never. The record follows what actually happened — always |

## Five sentences worth keeping in your pocket

1. "Walk me through your thinking." (the universal opener)
2. "What would you do if I weren't here?" (the flip that builds independence)
3. "Here's the bar; here's the gap; here's tomorrow's attempt." (fair feedback in one line)
4. "I'm not sure — let's find out together." (the credibility keeper)
5. "You're further along than you feel, and I can prove it." (the difficult-week anchor)
